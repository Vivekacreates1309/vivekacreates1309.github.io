Google Photos — finding the photo you only half remember

Users remember the story of a photo — the occasion, roughly when, who was there. Search only accepts its labels. The goal: raise the share of users who retrieve a photo they remember but can't precisely describe.

0 / 9
found their last half-remembered photo straight away
3 / 9
even tried typing it into search; 3 gave up and asked for a WhatsApp resend
145.8
RICE score of the chosen direction, vs 26.2 for a chatbot

How I got there. An LLM discovery engine classified Play Store, App Store and Reddit complaints into four retrieval break points — express, understand, evaluate, refine — ranked by frequency × severity × give-up rate. 15 user interviews and a survey validated it.

  • Episodic context engine splits a vague query into when, where, subject and vibe
  • Tappable probe chips replace the zero-result page
  • Before/after timeline strip lets users verify the match
  • North-star: 60%+ vague searches end in the target photo within 30 s; guardrails p95 latency 1.8 s, 92% top-3 relevance
Live MVP across 6 retrieval scenarios · tested with 3 users

VoicePad — why students type to ChatGPT when voice is faster

Students (18–24) are among ChatGPT's heaviest mobile users in India, yet rarely use voice input. The research showed the blocker wasn't awareness or accent — it was control.

84%
knew voice input existed — discovery isn't the problem
16%
used it frequently (n = 25)
40%
had never used it at all
"Typing feels easier because I can pause, think, edit and rephrase my question. With voice, I feel like I have to speak continuously." — student interviewee, Milestone 2 research

The bet — make speaking exactly as editable as typing. Chosen over contextual nudges and a voice-first mode on impact, effort and confidence.

  • Pause / resume / cancel while recording, with a live transcript
  • Mandatory review step — nothing auto-sends
  • "Did you mean?" flags driven by per-word speech-to-text confidence
  • Frequency-capped nudge triggered by typing friction; 7 analytics events and an A/B plan with a holdout
3-page PRD · clickable prototype built with Lovable

Wishlist Reveal — a return-trigger for Myntra's dead wishlists

Growth-team case study: Myntra users add items to their wishlist with real intent, then never come back before that intent fades. The brief — lift 30-day purchase conversion on wishlisted items, with no monetary discounts on the table.

of active wishlisters didn't buy within 30 days

Why the 14 non-buyers didn't buy (multi-select), from a 24-response survey; cross-validated against 8 interviews.

"Myntra's wishlist users lose real purchase intent not because they don't want the item, but because the wishlist gives them no reason to come back before that intent fades." — locked problem statement, from synthesis of interview + survey data

The MVP — "Wishlist Reveal." Instead of a flat price-drop alert, a curiosity-driven notification pulls users back at the moment their intent is most likely to reactivate.

  • Collection prompt + reminder at save-time, with a no-friction "skip to Uncategorized" path
  • Red-dot indicator plus price-drop, delivery-ETA and back-in-stock badges on wishlist items
  • Adaptive reminder that pulls itself forward when a delivery window demands it
  • "Simulate Next Visit" demo mode standing in for a live pricing/logistics feed
